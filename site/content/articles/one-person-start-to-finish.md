Most dealership visits pass through three or four people: whoever greets you,
whoever "handles the numbers", a manager, and a finance office. By the time
you reach the second one, the first conversation is already a memory. It is
how a purchase starts to feel like a process instead of a decision.

On this site there is one name on the deal, from the first question to the
keys.

## Who you are actually talking to

Ed Portello has been selling and supporting Cadillacs in South Charlotte
since 1999 — more than twenty-five years. The phone number on this page is
his, the inbox goes to him, and the car you are asking about is on the lot
he walks every day: 10725 Pineville Rd, a few minutes from South Charlotte.

When you ask whether an Escalade has the second-row bench or captain's
chairs, the answer comes from someone who can walk outside and check — not
from a call centre reading a screen.

## What that changes in practice

There are two kinds of questions in a car purchase: the ones about the car,
and the ones about the money. Both go to the same person here.

- **The car.** [Every Cadillac in stock](/inventory/) has photographs, the
  real mileage and the actual asking price. No "call for price", no car
  that sold last week still glowing on the page.
- **Your trade.** A [trade-in valuation](/trade-in/) takes a few minutes and
  gives you a real number to work from before the conversation starts.
- **The money.** [Financing questions](/financing/) — term lengths, what a
  payment looks like, what to bring — are answered by the person who will
  be standing at the desk with you, not handed off to an office you have
  never met.
